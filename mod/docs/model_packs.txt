Model packs
===========

A model pack replaces the game's 3D models (characters, zombies, bosses, props, scenery) without touching
pol.HAB. Each pack is a folder in models\:

    models\<pack>\<bank>\<model id>.obj     a Wavefront OBJ (with an optional .mtl next to it)
    models\<pack>\<bank>\<model id>.bin     a model in the game's own format (as dumped)

<bank> is optional (models\<pack>\<model id>.obj works too); model ids are unique across the game.
List the packs in todmod_config.exe ("Model packs", first wins) or in todmod.ini:

    [models]
    packs=mypack

Getting the originals
---------------------
- In game: tick "Save loaded models to models\dump" (todmod.ini [models] dump=1) and play. Every model the game
  loads is written to models\dump\<bank>\<model id>.obj/.mtl (for editing) and .bin (the original bytes).
  "List loaded models in todmod_models.log" ([debug] log_models=1) lists them with the time they loaded.
- All at once, with textures: tools\model_tool.py (Python 3 with numpy and Pillow):
      python tools\model_tool.py export "<game folder>" "<out folder>" [bank ...]
  writes every model of pol.HAB as OBJ + MTL + .bin and each bank's textures as PNG, plus models.tsv.
  python tools\model_tool.py preview <model.obj> <preview.png> draws a quick front and side view.

Editing an OBJ
--------------
- Coordinates are the game's (y up), with z negated so the file is right-handed like most editors expect.
  Keep the model's scale and origin; characters are made of many parts, each its own model id, posed by the
  game's animations.
- Materials say which texture and colours a part uses: "m<chunk>_t<texture>" takes the colours and render
  flags of material chunk <chunk> of the original model and draws with texture <texture> of the model's bank
  (textures\NNN.png in an export). "m<chunk>_none" draws without a texture. Other names use chunk 0.
  To repaint, copy the texture (textures\NNN_id<id>.png in an export) into a texture pack folder and edit it;
  it is matched by its texture id (textures\README.txt), also at 2, 4 or 8 times the size.
- Faces are triangulated; normals and texture coordinates are used when present (flat normals otherwise).
- New textures cannot be added yet: use the bank's own texture slots.

Your own character (one OBJ)
----------------------------
A character made as a single mesh (one OBJ with one texture, standing in a T-pose or A-pose, y up, facing +z,
as most model sites and editors export them) does not work dropped into a folder: the game needs one OBJ per
body part, named by model id, moved as rigid pieces. tools\obj_to_character.py (Python 3 with Pillow) cuts it up for you:

    python tools\obj_to_character.py "<folder>\Tommy.obj" . GTA 46 --name "Tommy Vercetti"

run in this game's folder, writes the pack "GTA" for character kind 46 (any kind from the Characters tab; several
kinds may follow). Tick the pack under "Model packs"; the Characters tab shows it under the name given, and every
character of that kind looks like it, and "Play as this" plays as it. Converting onto kind 57 (Player 1) or 58
(Player 2), e.g. "... GTA 57", replaces the player itself, with "Player 1 as" left on Normal. The cut follows standard body
proportions, so unusual shapes (long coats, big hair) may bend oddly.

By default the model stays one smooth mesh that bends at the joints (skinning): the converter writes the whole mesh
into the chest's model with a <model id>.skin file next to it (which part each corner follows, blended across the
joints), and todmod bends it every frame with the game's own animation. The other parts of the kind become empty
stand-ins. --rigid instead cuts the model into separate rigid pieces (as before 0.30.0; seams open at the joints).

Rigged characters (glTF / GLB)
------------------------------
A character that comes with its own skeleton and skin weights bends at its real joints. Export it as glTF 2.0
(.glb or .gltf; FBX and most other formats: import into Blender, then File > Export > glTF 2.0), standing in
a T-pose or A-pose, and run

    python tools\gltf_to_character.py "<folder>\hero.glb" . heroes 57 --name "Hero"

Bones are matched to the game's body parts by their names (Mixamo, Blender/Rigify, Biped, VRM and similar:
hips/pelvis, spine/chest, neck/head, upper arm, forearm, hand, thigh, calf/leg, foot); fingers, toes, twist and
other helper bones follow their parent. The tool lists which bones went to which part. Materials are packed into
one texture.

House of the Dead models
------------------------
If you own The House of the Dead (PC, 1998), its zombies can walk in this game. tools\hotd\ (Python 3 with
Pillow) reads them from your copy of that game:

    python tools\hotd\make_zombie_pack.py "<HOTD folder>\thotd.exe" "<HOTD folder>\art\stage1\creature" .

run in this game's folder, writes the pack "hotd_zombies": each of this game's 23 human zombie kinds becomes a
different House of the Dead zombie (body parts, damaged versions and head animation frames, with an atlas of
their textures). tools\hotd\make_human_pack.py writes "hotd_humans" the same way: the civilians and story
characters become the House of the Dead agents, Sophie, the scientists and the robed figure. Pick the packs under
"Model packs" in todmod_config.exe (both: hotd_zombies,hotd_humans). The Characters tab then shows them with
their names and previews, so you can also play as them ("Play as this"). One body onto chosen kinds:

    python tools\hotd\to_tod.py "<HOTD>\thotd.exe" "<HOTD>\art\stage1\creature" . <body> <kind> [<kind> ...]

(<body> is a House of the Dead body number, <kind> a character kind as on the Characters tab; pack
"hotd_body<body>"). tools\hotd\preview_tod.py draws a converted pack on its skeleton; tools\hotd\assemble.py and
export_parts.py export all House of the Dead bodies and parts as OBJ for other uses. The packs are made from your
own copy of the game: don't share them.

Notes
-----
- A model pack can name the characters it changes: characters.tsv in the pack folder, lines kind<TAB>name. The
  Characters tab shows those names and draws the pack's models while the pack is listed under "Model packs".
- A model pack can carry textures: textures\<pack> (same name as the model pack) is used automatically while the
  model pack is chosen.
- Characters with a replaced model don't get the game's waist "belt" (vertices the game moves at run time to close
  the gap between torso and hips, which only fit the original model).
- Replacements apply when the game loads a model (a stage or menu start), not while it is shown.
- todmod.log lists every replaced model ("models: <id> <- <file>").
